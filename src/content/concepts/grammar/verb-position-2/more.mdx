### Why "position", not "word"

Learners often count words and get confused. German counts blocks. "Jeden Samstagmorgen um neun" is four words, but it is one block, so it fills position 1 on its own. The verb still comes next:

Jeden Samstagmorgen um neun **gehe** ich schwimmen.

A good test: could you answer a question with the block alone? "Wann gehst du schwimmen?" "Jeden Samstagmorgen um neun." If yes, it's one block.

### Questions follow their own rules

W-questions keep the verb in position 2. The question word takes position 1: **Wo** wohnst du? **Was** machst du heute?

Yes/no questions are the one place where the verb comes first: **Kommst** du morgen? English needs "do" for this. German just moves the verb.

### What goes in position 1?

Usually the subject. Put something else first when you want to stress it, or when it links to what was just said:

– Was machst du am Wochenende?

– Am Samstag arbeite ich. Am Sonntag schlafe ich lange.

Starting with the time makes the answer flow. Both versions are correct, so pick whichever sounds more natural in context.

### The frame

Once you see the frame, long sentences get easier. Find the verb in position 2, then look at the very end. Together they tell you what is happening:

Ich **habe** am Wochenende mit meiner Schwester einen langen Spaziergang **gemacht**.

Everything in between is detail.
