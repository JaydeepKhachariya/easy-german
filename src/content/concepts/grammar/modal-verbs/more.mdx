### möchten or wollen?

Both mean you want something. **möchten** is softer and politer. Use it in shops, restaurants and with people you don't know: Ich möchte einen Tee, bitte.

**wollen** is direct. It's fine for plans and strong wishes: Ich will nächstes Jahr nach Deutschland ziehen. If you say "Ich will einen Kaffee" to a waiter, it sounds pushy.

### Rules and signs: man + modal

Signs and rules often use **man** (one, you, people) with a modal:

- Hier darf man nicht rauchen. (No smoking here.)
- Man muss das Ticket vorher kaufen. (You have to buy the ticket in advance.)
- Kann man hier mit Karte bezahlen? (Can you pay by card here?)

### müssen and dürfen with nicht

This trips up almost every English speaker, so it is worth a second look.

- Du **musst** nicht kommen. = You don't have to come. (It's your choice.)
- Du **darfst** nicht kommen. = You're not allowed to come.

English "must not" is a ban. German "muss nicht" isn't. If you want to forbid something, reach for dürfen.

### Dropping the main verb

When the meaning is clear, Germans leave out the main verb:

- Ich muss jetzt los. (I have to go now.)
- Kannst du Deutsch? (Do you speak German?)
- Ich möchte ein Glas Wasser. (I'd like a glass of water.)

You'll hear this all the time, so don't look for a missing word.
