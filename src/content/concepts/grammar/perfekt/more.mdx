### When fahren takes haben

fahren takes sein because someone moves. But when you drive a car (the car is a direct object), it takes haben:

- Ich **bin** nach Köln gefahren. (I went to Cologne.)
- Ich **habe** das Auto meines Vaters gefahren. (I drove my father's car.)

At A1 you'll mostly need the first one. The rule of thumb still holds: movement with no object means sein.

### Präteritum for sein and haben

A few verbs prefer the simple past even in conversation. The most common are **sein** and **haben**: Ich war krank. Wir hatten keine Zeit. You can say "Ich bin krank gewesen", but "Ich war krank" is more usual. The modals behave the same way (ich konnte, ich musste). There is a separate lesson on war and hatte.

### How to learn participles

Don't memorise long lists in one sitting. Learn each new verb as a set of three:

- fahren, fährt, ist gefahren
- essen, isst, hat gegessen
- kaufen, kauft, hat gekauft

The third form tells you two things at once: the participle and the helper. Put these sets on your review cards.

### Questions and the Perfekt

The frame stays the same in questions. Only position 1 changes:

- **Was** hast du gestern gemacht?
- **Hast** du schon gegessen?
